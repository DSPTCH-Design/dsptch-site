"How long will it take?" is one of the first questions every client asks, and it's a fair one. The honest answer depends less on how fast a studio works than on a few things that are mostly in your control.

## What sets the schedule

**Content readiness.** Copy and images are the most common bottleneck. A site can't be finished around placeholder text.

**Scope.** Five pages move faster than fifteen. Online stores, booking systems and integrations add time.

**Feedback.** One clear round of consolidated notes keeps things moving. Notes that trickle in from several people restart the clock.

**Decision-makers.** The more people who need to approve, the longer each step takes.

## A typical sequence

1. **Scope and kickoff.** Confirm pages, features and what's needed from you.
2. **Design direction.** Agree on the look before building every page.
3. **Build.** Responsive pages built to the approved direction.
4. **Review.** Consolidated feedback rounds.
5. **Testing and launch.** Every page checked on phone, tablet and desktop, then launched.

## How to keep your project on track

- Gather your copy, logo and photos before kickoff.
- Name one person to collect and send feedback.
- Block time on your calendar for each review.
- Keep new ideas for a second phase instead of adding them mid-build.

## The short version

If your content is ready and the scope is clear, a well-run website project moves quickly. Most delays come from waiting, not working.
