Website builders have made it easy to get something online. So when is it worth paying a designer? It depends on what your website needs to do.

## When a template makes sense

- You're just starting out and need a presence quickly.
- Your budget is very limited.
- Your offer is simple and easy to explain.
- You enjoy writing and updating the site yourself.

## When a designed site makes sense

- Clients compare you against competitors before they call.
- What you sell is expensive or considered, so trust matters.
- Your brand is part of your value.
- You need the site to explain something complicated clearly.

## What you're really paying for

The template versus designer debate often focuses on visuals. The bigger differences are underneath:

- **Strategy.** Deciding what the site is for and who it serves.
- **Messaging.** Saying what you do in words your clients use.
- **Hierarchy.** Putting the most important things where people look first.
- **Restraint.** Leaving out what doesn't help.

## The middle path

Fixed-scope website packages sit between DIY and a large agency project. You get a designed, custom site with a defined page count, clear revision rounds and a known price, without months of meetings.

## A simple rule

If your website is mostly a business card, a template is fine. If it's part of how you win work, design it.
