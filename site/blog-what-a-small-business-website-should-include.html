What should a small business website include?

All articles Draft
Short answer

A strong small business website needs a clear homepage, a page for each core service, proof of your work, an about page, an easy way to get in touch, and solid technical foundations: fast loading, mobile-friendly layouts, page titles and descriptions, and a working contact form.

Most visitors decide within a few seconds whether a business looks credible. Your website's job is to answer their questions quickly and make the next step obvious.

The pages most businesses need

A homepage that says what you do, for whom. Lead with a plain statement of your offer, not a slogan. Follow it with proof and a clear call to action.

One page per core service. Each service page should explain who it's for, what's included, how it works and what it costs or what affects the cost. Separate pages also give search engines and AI assistants a clear page to point to for each service.

Proof. Project examples, case studies, client logos or testimonials, shown with permission. Real examples persuade more than any claim.

About. People buy from people. Introduce the team, your experience and how you work.

Contact. A short form, an email address and, where relevant, your location and hours.

The features that matter

  • Mobile-friendly layouts. Many of your visitors will arrive on a phone.
  • Fast loading. Optimized images and lean pages keep people from leaving.
  • A working lead form. Test it before launch and make sure submissions reach the right inbox.
  • Page titles and descriptions. These shape how your pages appear in search results and link previews.
  • Analytics. You can't improve what you don't measure.

What you can leave out at launch

Complex animations, large image carousels and features nobody asked for add cost and slow pages down. Launch with what your customers need, then add to it based on what you learn.

A simple test

Ask someone outside your business to find three things on your site: what you do, an example of your work, and how to contact you. If any of them takes more than ten seconds, that's where to start.

Frequently asked questions

How many pages does a small business website need?

Most small businesses are well served by five to ten pages. A homepage, one page per core service, a work or results page, an about page and a contact page cover what most visitors look for.

Do I need a blog?

Not to launch. A blog helps when you can publish useful answers to real customer questions on a regular schedule. If you can't keep it current, start without one.

Should my prices be on my website?

If your services have predictable scope, showing a starting price filters out poor-fit inquiries and builds trust. If every project is custom, explain what affects the price instead.
